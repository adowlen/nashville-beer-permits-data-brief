# Reviewer notes - Nashville Beer Permits brief (2026-11-06)

Claims most likely to be challenged:

1. **"22.2 percent in District 19"** - a direct count of the register's own Council_Dist field (458 of 2,064 issued; 8 records carry no district). If a reader's district map differs from the register's field, counts will differ; the brief attributes the geography to the register itself.
2. **The entered-to-issued gaps** - Date_Issued behaves in part as a last-issuance stamp (batch evidence in the audit). The brief prints this warning in the section head and on the lag chart, and headlines only the entered-2023+ cohort median (96 days). A challenger could still read the all-years subtype medians as application waits; they are labeled as register shape, not waits.
3. **The police join** - 46.6 percent of incident records have no numeric ZIP, and ZIP completeness may vary by report type, so the share comparison is printed with that limit and a no-causality note. The Spearman figure (0.904, 33 ZIPs) is a rank correlation of shares, not of counts.
4. **Permits are not venues** - the Gaylord control (74 permits, one complex) is printed in the brief itself so the distinction cannot be missed.

Methods checks that could not be completed:

- **Owner-level concentration:** Business_Owner is blank on 51.2 percent of records, so no owner figure is published. Recorded here as an incomplete check by design.
- **Per-capita district rates:** no district population denominator was pulled for this brief; district findings are shares of the register only, and the brief says so.

Findings that surprised us:

- The register's TEMPORARY side is only 53 records and all of them are recent (entered on or after 2025-03-13) - there is no visible older pending backlog in the current-status list.
- A single resort complex accounts for a third of the permits in the register's second-largest ZIP.
- Nothing in this brief reflects badly on a specific agency, department, or named official beyond the published register's own field completeness, which is stated as a records-practice observation with an outcome measure.
